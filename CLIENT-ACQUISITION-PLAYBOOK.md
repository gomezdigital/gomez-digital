# Gomez Digital — Client Acquisition Playbook

Internal working document. This is an operating guide, not a public-facing page.

## Positioning

**Gomez Digital helps small businesses and creators present their offer clearly online through mobile-first websites, landing pages and practical digital systems.**

Brand promise: **Digital Experiences That Move.**

Keep the visual identity consistent: near-black, charcoal, white and warm orange; GD monogram; Space Grotesk + DM Sans; direct, premium, helpful language.

## Initial market

Start with English-speaking US businesses for outbound testing, while keeping the offer open to Botswana and worldwide clients. Do not target every industry at once. Begin with owner-operated service businesses and creators where a website can help a visitor understand the offer and take a clear next step.

Useful prospect signals:
- No website linked from their public business profile.
- A website that is difficult to use on a phone.
- No clear service explanation, enquiry path or call to action.
- A mismatch between their active social presence and their website.
- A new offer or business launch that needs a focused landing page.

Only mention problems you personally checked. Never invent performance data, search rankings, testimonials or results.

## The offer

### Complimentary 3-point website review
Review three things:
1. Clarity: can a new visitor quickly understand the offer?
2. Mobile journey: is the page readable and easy to navigate on a phone?
3. Next step: can the visitor easily enquire, book or request a quote?

Send a short written review with two or three practical suggestions. It is a conversation starter, not a promise of higher rankings or revenue.

### Paid project options
- Business website: a clear, mobile-first online home.
- Landing page: a focused page for one offer, campaign or service.
- Website refresh: improvements to an existing site.
- Ongoing support: updates and maintenance agreed separately.

Quote each project only after agreeing scope, pages, features, responsibilities, timing and revisions. Put the agreement in writing and request an upfront deposit before starting. Keep domains, third-party subscriptions and ongoing maintenance clearly separate from the build fee.

## Prospecting routine (phone-friendly)

Aim for a consistent, manageable daily block rather than bulk generic messages.

1. Find 5 relevant businesses through public business listings, search or professional social profiles.
2. Open each website on your phone and identify one real, specific improvement.
3. Record the business name, website, public contact channel, observation, date contacted and next follow-up date.
4. Send a brief personalized message.
5. Follow up once after several business days if appropriate; stop if they decline or ask not to be contacted.
6. Deliver any promised review promptly and keep the next step clear.

Track: **Prospect → Researched → Contacted → Replied → Review sent → Conversation → Proposal → Won / Lost / Follow up later.**

## When someone replies positively

Ask:
- What is the main goal of the website?
- Who is the target customer?
- Is there an existing website or preferred reference?
- What pages or features are essential?
- Is there a target launch date?
- Who will supply copy, photos and other content?

Then summarize the scope in writing, quote the work, state what is and is not included, and agree on a deposit and milestones before starting.

## Proof and trust

- Link to the live Anne's Cash Loans website as a client project.
- Label Hustla Mindset and Gomez Alpha accurately as projects/concepts where appropriate; do not imply they are paid client work.
- Ask satisfied clients for permission to publish a genuine testimonial and project summary.
- Build case studies around the problem, scope, decisions and delivered work. Add results only when the client has verified them.
- Do not promise first-page Google rankings, guaranteed sales, or guaranteed conversion improvements.

## Weekly scorecard

Every week record:
- Relevant prospects researched
- Personalized messages sent
- Replies received
- Website reviews delivered
- Discovery conversations
- Quotes sent
- Projects won
- Most common objections

Use the results to improve targeting and the offer. If replies are low, check lead relevance and personalization before increasing message volume.

## Search visibility checklist

- Keep the sitemap current when public pages are added.
- Check that each public page has a unique title, description and canonical URL.
- Verify mobile rendering and page links after each deployment.
- In Google Search Console, inspect the homepage and the new website design page, run the live URL test, and request indexing when available.
- Check the sitemap report after submission. A sitemap helps discovery but does not guarantee indexing or rankings.
